About CISCM

A professional institute.
A practical standard.

CISCM is a Texas-registered independent professional organization focused on demonstrable competence in supply chain, logistics, procurement and operations.

What credible practice should demonstrate.

CISCM connects professional standards with learning, assessment, membership, accreditation and continuing development. Its work serves individuals, employers, education providers and corporate academies.

The Body of Knowledge describes domains, competency units, performance criteria and proficiency levels. These support evidence-based decisions about capability and learning quality.

  • Standards and job-relevant learning
  • Assessment and professional recognition
  • Continuing professional development
  • Institutional and program quality

Our direction.

01

Vision

Support capable, responsible, efficient and future-ready supply chain professionals.

02

Mission

Define and assess competence, develop people and organizations, recognize credible learning systems and promote ethical improvement.

Operating values

Values made visible in practice.

01

Practical relevance

Address real decisions, workplace outputs and operating consequences.

02

Integrity and accurate claims

Describe recognition precisely and within the scope formally approved.

03

Competence and evidence

Use current, verifiable evidence rather than attendance or reputation alone.

04

Stakeholder protection

Maintain fair and transparent information, assessment, records and complaints arrangements.

05

Accessibility and fairness

Support valid preparation routes and reasonable adjustments without changing the competence standard.

06

Industry currency

Respond to changes in technology, trade, regulation, sustainability and professional practice.

07

Impartiality

Separate commercial interests from recognition and complaint outcomes.

08

Continuous improvement

Use performance data, feedback, monitoring and concerns to improve practice.

Learning routes that fit professional life.

Classroom, live virtual, online, blended and workplace learning can all support competence. Effective learning connects concepts with decisions, tools, cases, calculations, simulations and evidence of application.

Preparation may combine employment, self-directed study, academic learning, employer development and approved providers, subject to the relevant credential rules.

Governance and accountability.

  • Recommended separation of policy, technical review, administration and decision-making
  • Quality assurance, assessor and authorized certification responsibilities
  • Independent appeal arrangements and controlled credential issuance
Recognition should be useful, specific and verifiable. The formal decision establishes the scope, validity and continuing obligations.
