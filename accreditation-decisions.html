Accreditation Decisions

Evidence supports judgement.
Scores do not replace it.

Understand how evidence strength and nonconformity inform a controlled recognition decision.

70CISCM

How strong is the evidence?

01

Strong

Current sources agree and show consistent implementation, monitoring and corrective action.

02

Adequate

Relevant evidence demonstrates requirements with limited gaps or sampling uncertainty.

03

Weak

Evidence relies on policy or assertion, with recent, isolated or inconsistent implementation.

04

Unreliable

Evidence conflicts, lacks attribution, appears altered or cannot be verified.

Indicative institutional scoring.

01

70% overall

Recommended overall minimum for institutional scoring.

01

60% per standard

Recommended minimum in every applicable standard.

These are recommended decision rules. Unconditional approval also requires no critical nonconformity and no unresolved major nonconformity. Scores do not override serious weakness in legality, learner protection, assessment, data, certification or truthful claims.

Classifying nonconformity.

01

Critical

Immediate or severe risk to legality, safety, integrity, data, learners or public trust.

02

Major

A systemic or material failure affecting reliability within the approved scope.

03

Minor

A limited weakness requiring correction without, by itself, undermining the system.

04

Observation

An opportunity to strengthen practice without a nonconformity finding.

Payment and decision independence.

Fees purchase the defined review or service, not approval. Sales personnel, agents, consultants and partners must not promise accreditation, a particular grade or a passing result.
